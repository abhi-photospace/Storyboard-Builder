# 🎬 Abhinandan's Storyboard Builder

**A free, open-source, browser-based storyboarding tool for filmmakers.**

Drag in your frames, add shot details, sketch over images, present your storyboard, and export clean PDFs or shot lists, all without Photoshop templates, subscriptions, or sign-ups.

## 💡 Why this app exists

Before every shoot, there is pre-production. And for a lot of film students, pre-production means dragging drawn storyboards and location photos into Photoshop, lining everything up by hand, and adding text layer after text layer for every shot.

The storyboard sites out there were either paid, limited, or didn't match the template that was actually needed. So this app was built to do exactly that job: **import your frames, fill in your shot details, export a clean, professional storyboard.** Fast.

## 🚀 Getting started

There is nothing to install.

1. **Download** the app file, **`Abhinandan's Storyboard Builder_v2.6.3.zip`**, from this repository.
2. **Extract** the contents from the downloaded .zip file (`Abhinandan's Storyboard Builder_v2.6.3.html`).
3. **Open it in your browser** by double-clicking the file (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari on a desktop or laptop works best).
4. Wait 2 to 3 seconds for the loading screen, which shows the app name and the developer's contact details.
5. You land on a blank project. Start building.

> 💻 **Best on desktop.** Hover descriptions (small tooltips that explain each button) only appear on desktop, where there is a mouse to hover with.

## 💾 Managing your project

Your storyboard is saved as a **project file** on your own computer. You stay in control of your work.

### New project
Starts a fresh, empty storyboard. Shortcut: **Ctrl + N**

> ⚠️ Save your current work first if you want to keep it.

### Save project
Downloads your entire storyboard (frames, notes, settings) as a project file. Shortcut: **Ctrl + S**

### Import project
Loads a project file you saved earlier, so you can pick up exactly where you left off. Shortcut: **Ctrl + O**

> Import project was previously called "Open project". Same thing, clearer name.

### The golden rule
**Save often.** Your project file is your backup. Save before closing the tab, before refreshing, and before switching computers.

**Working with a teammate?** Save your project and send them the file. They can load it with *Import project*.

## 🎞️ Building your storyboard

Your storyboard is organised into **Scenes**, **Pages**, and **Shots**.

### Scene, page, and shot numbers
Every panel carries numbers so your whole crew stays on the same page.

- **Scene numbers accept letters**, so you can use `1A`, `3B`, `12C` and so on. This is handy for scenes that get split or added late.
- **Page and shot numbers** keep your sequence tidy and are carried into your exports.

### Adding shots
Add a new shot panel to your scene, then drop an image into it and fill in the details.

### Rearranging shots
Each shot has a **drag handle (⋮)** beside its shot number.

1. Click and hold the three dots.
2. Drag the shot to its new position.
3. Release.

**How it works:** the shot you drag is **inserted** at the new spot and the other shots **shift** to make room. Nothing gets swapped or overwritten, and the shot numbers follow the new order.

## 📝 Adding shot details

Each shot panel has fields for the information your DOP, director, and crew need.

### Core fields
- **Shot type**: Wide, Medium, Close-up, and so on.
- **Lens**: for example 24mm, 50mm, 85mm.
- **Notes**: action, blocking, dialogue, mood, anything you want to say.

### Optional fields
Switch these on only when a shot needs them:
- **Camera** (movement, angle, support)
- **Lighting**
- **Sound**

### Custom fields
Need something that isn't listed, such as VFX, props, or costume? Add your own **custom field** and name it whatever you like.

> ✅ **Shot type matters.** Before exporting, the app checks that every shot has a shot type (see [Exporting](#-exporting)).

## 🖼️ Working with images

### Adding an image
**Drag and drop** an image straight onto a shot panel. This can be a drawn frame, a location photo, a reference still, or a screenshot.

### Fill mode and focal point
In **Fill mode**, the image fills the whole panel and may get cropped to fit. You decide which part stays in view:

1. Switch the image to **Fill** mode.
2. **Click and drag inside the image** to reposition its focal point.
3. Stop when the important part of the frame is where you want it.

This is perfect for keeping a character's face or a key object from being cropped out.

## ✏️ Sketching and annotating

You don't need a finished drawing to plan a frame. The built-in **sketch and annotation tools** work **much like MS Paint**: pick a tool, pick a colour, and draw straight onto your frame. If you've ever doodled in Paint, you already know how to use them.

Use them to:

- Draw rough compositions directly in a panel.
- Mark camera moves, blocking, and eyelines on top of a photo or frame.
- Add quick visual notes that words can't capture.

## 🖥️ Presentation mode

Want to walk your team or faculty through the storyboard? Switch on **Presentation mode** to view your frames full-screen, one at a time, without the editing clutter. It works great for pitches, assessments, and shoot briefings.

## 📤 Exporting

### Export to PDF
Creates a print-ready storyboard document.

- **Paper size:** Choose your paper size before exporting. **A4 is the default.**
- **Layout:** Each page uses a clean **3-row portrait layout**: the **image on the left** and the **notes on the right** for every shot.

### The missing shot type warning
If any shot is missing a **shot type**, the app warns you before exporting.

- The warning **appears every single time** you export, until every shot has a shot type.
- If you really want to go ahead, choose **Export anyway**.
- Otherwise, fix the shots and export again.

### Export shot list
Generates a clean **shot list** from your storyboard, useful for the shoot day and for your DOP and AD.

## 🌟 Tips and good habits

- **Save early, save often.** Use Ctrl + S every time you finish a scene.
- **Set shot types as you go** to avoid the export warning at the end.
- **Use lettered scene numbers** (`4A`, `4B`) instead of renumbering everything when a scene splits.
- **Use Fill mode and the focal point** for photos with a clear subject, so nothing important gets cropped.
- **Name your project files clearly**, for example `Stairway-Scene_v3.json`, so you can find the right version later.
- **Do a test export early.** Check your layout and paper size well before the deadline.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting / FAQ

**Do I need an account or internet connection?**
No account is needed. The app runs in your browser, and your projects live on your own computer.

**I closed the tab and my work disappeared.**
Always use **Save project** before leaving. Your saved project file is the safe copy. Load it again with **Import project**.

**The hover descriptions aren't showing.**
They are desktop-only. They need a mouse pointer.

**The export warning keeps appearing.**
At least one shot has no shot type. Fill them all in, or choose **Export anyway**.

**A dragged shot moved somewhere I didn't expect.**
Dragging inserts the shot at the drop position and shifts the others along. Drag it again to the position you want.

**Part of my image is cropped in the panel.**
Use **Fill mode** and drag inside the image to move the focal point.

**Which paper sizes can I export?**
A4 is the default. Other sizes are available in the paper size option before export.
